# I-95 state by state

Status: design reference, written 2026-11-10 from general knowledge. Every number is checked against current sources when that state is built (M10). Florida has its own research notes in VISION.md.

I-95 runs about 1,900 miles from Miami to Houlton, Maine, where it ends at the Canadian border. It crosses 15 states, plus a few hundred feet of Washington, D.C. on the Woodrow Wilson Bridge.

## Weather is a per-run draw, by season
A run takes place in one season. Each state has odds for each kind of weather in that season. Rare events stay rare. A hurricane evacuation or a blizzard should happen in only a small share of runs, and the game warns you on the radio before it hits.

| Weather | What it does to driving |
|---|---|
| Sun and glare | Low sun at dawn and dusk blinds you facing east or west. Kept subtle |
| Heat and humidity | Fogged windshield when the A/C kicks on, heat shimmer, overheating on old cars |
| Pop-up thunderstorm | Sudden downpour, a few minutes of near-zero visibility, hydroplaning, then sun again |
| Lightning | Flashes and close strikes. Some drivers pull over under overpasses and block the shoulder |
| Fog | Short sight lines and chain-reaction crashes. Inland morning fog in the Southeast |
| Smoke | Brush fire smoke crossing the road (Florida, Georgia). Visibility drops in seconds |
| Tropical storm and hurricane | Wind gusts that push high-sided vehicles, flooding, contraflow evacuation traffic heading north |
| Flash flood | Water across low lanes. Stall the engine if you drive in too deep |
| Tornado | Rare. Warning on the radio, debris, a closed road |
| Nor'easter | Wind-driven rain or snow along the coast from Virginia north |
| Snow | Lower grip, plowed and unplowed lanes, plows and salt trucks, cars in the median |
| Freezing rain and black ice | Looks dry, isn't. Bridges freeze first |
| Blizzard | Whiteout, closures, convoys behind plows. Maine and New England, winter only |

## The states, south to north

| State | Typical I-95 limit | Weather that matters | Police and law | Tolls | Road character |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Florida | 55-65 urban, 70 rural | Lightning, pop-up storms, hurricanes, glare, brush-fire smoke, fog | FHP, aircraft speed enforcement, Move Over | 95 Express lanes (Miami) | Dense, fast and chaotic in Miami, long flat runs north of West Palm Beach. Retirees, tourists, lifted trucks |
| Georgia | 70 rural, lower near Savannah and Brunswick | Thunderstorms, heat, fog, hurricanes on the coast | GSP: aggressive, quick to PIT. "Super speeder" fee for 85+ | None on I-95 | Short (about 112 miles), marshes and bridges, heavy truck traffic from the Savannah port |
| South Carolina | 70 | Hurricanes, flash floods, tornadoes, thunderstorms, fog | SC Highway Patrol | None | Long rural stretch, billboards (South of the Border), many truckers, few services |
| North Carolina | 70, 65 in places | Hurricanes, flash floods, tornadoes, ice storms inland in winter | NC State Highway Patrol | None yet | Rural, flat, construction for widening, notorious summer beach traffic |
| Virginia | 70 rural, 55-60 near Richmond and D.C. | Fog, ice storms, snow in the north, thunderstorms | Reckless driving is 20+ over or over 85 mph, a criminal charge. Strict with speeders | Express lanes in northern Virginia | Turns into the D.C. crawl. Some of the worst traffic in the country |
| Washington, D.C. | 55 | As Virginia and Maryland | D.C. police on the bridge | None | A few hundred feet on the Woodrow Wilson Bridge |
| Maryland | 65, lower around Baltimore | Snow, ice, nor'easters, thunderstorms | Maryland State Police. Speed cameras in work zones | Fort McHenry Tunnel, JFK Highway toll | Baltimore tunnels, heavy trucks, aggressive merging |
| Delaware | 65 | Snow, ice, nor'easters | Delaware State Police, known for a speed trap reputation | Newark toll plaza (one of the priciest short tolls) | Very short (about 23 miles), crowded |
| Pennsylvania | 55 in Philadelphia, higher elsewhere | Snow, ice, thunderstorms | Pennsylvania State Police | None on I-95 itself | Philadelphia waterfront, potholes, construction |
| New Jersey | 65 on the Turnpike | Nor'easters, snow, ice | NJ State Police on the Turnpike | NJ Turnpike tolls | Turnpike with split car and truck lanes, refineries, nonstop merging |
| New York | 50 in the city | Snow, nor'easters, freezing rain | NYPD and NY State Police | George Washington Bridge, others | Gridlock in the Bronx, horns, the Cross Bronx Expressway |
| Connecticut | 55-65 | Snow, ice, nor'easters | CT State Police | None | Narrow, packed coastal corridor. Stamford to New Haven never moves |
| Rhode Island | 55-65 | Snow, nor'easters, hurricanes (rare) | RI State Police | None | Short, through Providence |
| Massachusetts | 65 | Heavy snow, nor'easters, black ice | Massachusetts State Police | None on I-95 (the Mass Pike crosses it) | Route 128 ring around Boston, aggressive Boston drivers |
| New Hampshire | 65 | Snow, nor'easters | NH State Police | Hampton toll plaza | Very short (about 16 miles), coastal |
| Maine | 70-75 north of Bangor | Heavy snow, blizzards, black ice, fog on the coast | Maine State Police | Maine Turnpike tolls | Empty, forested, moose crossings (moose collisions are deadly), ends at the Canadian border in Houlton |

## Ideas that come from this
- **Seasons:** Maine without snow would feel wrong. A winter run is a different game from a summer run.
- **Smuggler scenario:** I-95 ends at a real border crossing, so a north-bound smuggling run has a natural finish line.
- **Regional flavor:** each state needs at least one memorable thing: Florida lightning, Georgia troopers, the South of the Border billboards, the Virginia reckless-driving trap, the D.C. crawl, the Delaware toll, the Turnpike, the Cross Bronx, Boston drivers, Maine moose.
